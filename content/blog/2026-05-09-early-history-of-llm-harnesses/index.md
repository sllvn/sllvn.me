Recently, the buzz around LLMs has shifted from the models themselves (the intelligence) to the harnesses (the scaffolding). The "harness" is the tools, runtime, and instructions that turn an LLM into an agent. Things like "skills" and "memory" are all provided by the harness, sent as text into the model. For a while the engineering community was focused on "prompt engineering", then "agentic engineering", but in the past month, it's turned almost entirely to "harness engineering". [1]

### History

While there were earlier implementations of agentic loops, Claude Code (Feb 2025) was the first popular tool to get people thinking about harnesses, though the word wasn't yet in use. Claude Code unveiled the utility of the right scaffolding. OpenAI's Codex (May 2025) and open source harnesses like Opencode (June 2025) were quick to follow, but Claude Code had first-mover advantage and [it was a better product: both in output quality and experience]. Low-friction, local tools with a system prompt tuned for a specific use case (code) connected to frontier models proved very effective.

In Dec 2025, OpenClaw (originally "Clawdbot") brought agents to the masses: what happens if instead of limiting to coding tools you let the model have access to everything: your email, your contacts, your data, your computer? It was a security nightmare but the minimal friction showed the power of personal agents. The project took off like [wildfire](https://www.star-history.com/?repos=openclaw%2Fopenclaw&type=date&legend=top-left), with Mac Minis remaining difficult to purchase 5 months later: everybody wants to run their own agent against a local LLM.

In Feb 2026, the term "harness" became popular through an [OpenAI blog post](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/) and again when an OpenAI engineer shared his thinking: [Codex (an agent) = Model + Harness + Surfaces (runtime + interface)](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-i-think-codex-gabriel-chua-ukhic/).

In March 2026, Anthropic dropped the ball. Coinciding with scaling challenges (following positive PR generated by their [public spat with the US Dept. of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic%E2%80%93United_States_Department_of_Defense_dispute)), they began to lockdown their harness. They first blocked third-party use of their subscription plans, then introduced several bugs into their harness increasing token consumption (while remaining notably silent, fueling rumors), alongside billing changes. OpenAI benefited from Anthropic's crisis and many developers jumped ship, realizing that Codex had caught up, both as a harness and as a model.

Today (May 2025), all the energy is about harnesses: everybody is writing pieces on harness engineering (Osmani, Fowler), there are even [harness meta-frameworks](TODO)... I've even realized my own project is a harness, but at the core the idea remains the same: model + tools + instructions, running in a loop.
